# Code of Conduct

This code sets the standard of behaviour in SoAI project spaces and explains how the maintainer handles reports when someone falls short of it.

## Where it applies

The code covers issues and pull request threads in the SoAI GitHub repositories, as well as email exchanges with the project. It also applies to anyone acting on behalf of SoAI, and to behaviour elsewhere that targets someone because they take part in the project.

## Expected behaviour

- Stay polite when you disagree, and criticise code, designs, and decisions rather than the people behind them.
- Report what you observed, and say so when you are unsure about part of it.
- Search existing issues before opening a new one, then keep the discussion on that issue's topic.
- Be patient with people who are new to SoAI or who write in English as a second language.
- Remove personal information, credentials, and private messages from logs and screenshots before you share them.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, threats, or repeated contact after someone has asked you to stop.
- Insults or demeaning comments about a person's age, disability, ethnicity, nationality, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, religion, appearance, or level of experience.
- Sexual language or images, and unwanted sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Spam, advertising, and mass posting.
- Trolling, deliberately provoking arguments, or using another account to get around a moderation decision.
- Encouraging other people to do any of these things.

You can disagree with any project decision, including decisions about licensing and contributions, as long as you stay civil and do not post the same complaint repeatedly.

## Reporting a problem

Email `info@soai.to` with the subject `SoAI conduct report`. Describe what happened and who was involved, and include links or screenshots when you have them. You can report behaviour even when it was not directed at you.

The maintainer keeps reports confidential as far as the situation allows and does not reveal who made a report to the person reported without the reporter's agreement. If your report concerns the maintainer, say so in the email.

Security vulnerabilities do not go through this process. Report them privately as described in `SECURITY.md`.

## What happens after a report

The maintainer's response depends on how serious the behaviour was and whether it has happened before. The possible responses are:

1. A private message explaining which part of this code was broken.
2. A public warning, together with editing, hiding, or locking the content involved.
3. A temporary block on commenting or opening issues.
4. A permanent ban from project spaces.

Threats, harassment, and publishing private information can lead directly to a ban without an earlier warning. You can appeal a decision once by emailing `info@soai.to` and explaining why the decision should change.
